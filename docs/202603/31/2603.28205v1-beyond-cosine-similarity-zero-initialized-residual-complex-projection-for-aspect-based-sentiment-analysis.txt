# ABSTRACT 

Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA) is fundamentally challenged by representation entangle-ment, where aspect semantics and sentiment polarities are often conflated in real-valued embedding spaces. Furthermore, standard contrastive learning suffers from false-negative collisions, severely degrading performance on high-frequency aspects. In this paper, we propose a novel framework featuring a Zero-Initialized Residual Complex Projection (ZRCP) and an Anti-collision Masked Angle Loss,inspired by quantum projection and entanglement ideas. Our approach projects textual features into a complex semantic space, systematically utilizing the phase to disentangle sentiment polarities while allowing the amplitude to encode the semantic intensity and lexical richness of subjective descriptions. To tackle the collision bottleneck, we introduce an anti-collision mask that elegantly preserves intra-polarity aspect cohesion while expanding the inter-polarity discriminative margin by over 50%. Experimental results demonstrate that our framework achieves a state-of-the-art Macro-F1 score of 0.8851. Deep geometric analyses further reveal that explicitly penalizing the complex amplitude catastrophically over-regularizes subjective representations, proving that our unconstrained-amplitude and phase-driven objective is crucial for robust, fine-grained sentiment disentanglement.

# 1 Introduction 

Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA) is a fundamental yet highly challenging task in Natural Language Processing (NLP), aiming to extract specific aspect categories and their corresponding sentiment polarities from unstructured reviews. In real-world scenarios, a single sentence often contains complex semantic structures and multiple aspect-sentiment pairs. Consequently, mapping these diverse expressions into standard real-valued embedding spaces frequently leads to representation entanglement , where the objective aspect semantics and the subjective sentiment polarities are heavily conflated [1][2]. Recent advancements have leveraged contrastive learning, such as InfoNCE, to carve out better discriminative margins in the feature space[3]. However, we observe that applying standard contrastive learning directly to fine-grained ABSA tasks paradoxically induces severe false-negative collisions . Standard contrastive objectives predominantly rely on instance discrimination, which erroneously repels different sentences even if they share the exact same aspect and sentiment polarity. For instance, two distinct positive reviews about "Taste" are forcefully pushed apart in the vector space, catastrophically destroying the essential intra-aspect cohesion and blurring the decision boundary. To understand why complex-valued geometry offers a natural fit for ABSA, consider the intrinsic properties of aspect-level sentiments. Sentiment polarities—positive, negative, and neutral—are inherently directional and oppositional, making them well-suited to be represented by angles in a vector space. In contrast, the intensity or richness of a

Real-valued embeddings conflate these two orthogonal attributes into a single scalar dimension. Complex numbers, however, naturally decouple them: the phase can encode polarity, while the amplitude can encode intensity. This geometric intuition motivates us to design a projection module that maps real-valued features into a complex space, where sentiment and semantics can be disentangled at the representation level. To resolve this representation bottleneck, we propose a novel framework that systematically decouples aspect and senti-ment using complex-valued geometry. Specifically, we introduce the Zero-Initialized Residual Complex Projection (ZRCP) , which smoothly maps pre-trained real-valued features into a complex semantic space[5]. In this complex plane, we hypothesize and empirically prove a profound geometric property: the phase (angle) effectively isolates the subjective sentiment polarity, while the amplitude (magnitude) naturally encodes the semantic intensity and lexical richness of the description. Building upon this complex representation, we design an Anti-collision Masked Angle Loss . By introducing a dynamic mask matrix, our loss function strategically prevents the erroneous repulsion of same-polarity instances within the same aspect cluster. This enables the model to maximize the inter-polarity angular margin without sacrificing the global aspect cohesion. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • We propose a novel ZRCP module that elegantly projects textual embeddings into a complex space, providing a mathematical foundation for disentangling objective aspects and subjective polarities. • We introduce an Anti-collision Masked Angle Loss that solves the false-negative collision problem inherent in standard contrastive learning, expanding the inter-polarity discriminative margin by over 50% without destroying structural cohesion. • We conduct deep geometric failure analyses, revealing that explicitly penalizing the complex amplitude catastrophically over-regularizes the vector space. We prove that preserving natural amplitude variance is critical for modeling the intensity of subjective sentiments. • Extensive experiments demonstrate that our framework achieves a state-of-the-art Macro-F1 score of 0.8851, significantly outperforming robust baselines on high-frequency aspects.

# 2 Related Work 

2.1 Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis 

Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA) is a fine-grained sentiment classification task that requires the model to identify the sentiment polarity towards specific aspect terms or categories within a sentence [6]. Early approaches heavily relied on syntactic parsing and rule-based feature engineering [7]. With the advent of deep learning, neural architectures such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), and Attention mechanisms became the dominant paradigms, significantly improving contextual modeling [8]. Recently, Pre-trained Language Models (PLMs) like BERT and RoBERTa have achieved remarkable success in ABSA by leveraging massive amounts of pre-training data [9, 10]. Despite their strong performance, these models inherently map textual inputs into a continuous, real-valued high-dimensional space. In such spaces, objective aspect semantics (e.g., "Taste", "Decoration") and subjective sentiment polarities (e.g., "Positive", "Negative") are often heavily conflated. This representation entanglement restricts the model’s capacity to draw crisp decision boundaries for highly subjective expressions. 

2.2 Contrastive Learning in NLP 

Contrastive learning has emerged as a powerful technique for learning discriminative sentence representations. Methods like SimCSE [11] and its supervised variants have demonstrated that optimizing the InfoNCE loss can effectively pull semantically similar sentences together while pushing apart dissimilar ones, creating a more uniform and aligned embedding space. However, directly applying standard contrastive learning to fine-grained ABSA introduces a critical bottleneck: the 

false-negative collision problem. In aspect-level datasets, sentences frequently share the exact same aspect category and sentiment polarity. Standard contrastive objectives, which are primarily designed for instance discrimination, erroneously treat these same-class samples in a batch as negative pairs, forcing them apart [12]. This aggressive repulsion destroys intra-aspect cohesion and causes spatial collapse. Unlike previous works that attempt to re-weight negative samples, our framework introduces a dynamic Anti-collision Mask that structurally preserves essential semantic cohesion while expanding the inter-polarity margin. 

2.3 Complex-Valued Representations 

Complex-valued neural networks and quantum-inspired representations have recently gained traction in NLP due to their superior capacity to model uncertainties and complex feature interactions [13, 14]. Complex numbers inherently possess two degrees of freedom: the real part and the imaginary part (or alternatively, amplitude and phase). Prior works have explored complex-valued word embeddings to capture polysemy and order dependency [5]. Recent advancements have demonstrated the efficacy of mapping embeddings into a complex space for angle opti-mization. Notably, AnglE [15] introduced a framework that optimizes angular margins in complex space for general text embedding tasks, achieving significant improvements in semantic textual similarity. However, AnglE employs a direct hard chunking strategy that bisects the hidden state into real and imaginary parts, which can disrupt pre-trained semantic continuity when applied to fine-grained ABSA tasks. However, the geometric properties of complex spaces have rarely been exploited for fine-grained sentiment disen-tanglement. While previous efforts focused on complex-valued transformations, they often overlooked the explicit semantic roles of amplitude and phase[16]. In this paper, we propose a Zero-Initialized Residual Complex Projection (ZRCP) module. We move beyond simply projecting features into a complex space by empirically proving a profound geometric intuition: the phase acts as an ideal differentiator for sentiment polarity, while the amplitude naturally captures the lexical richness and intensity of subjective descriptions. This fundamental geometric decoupling fundamentally mitigates the entanglement issue in traditional real-valued spaces. While prior works have explored complex-valued embeddings for capturing polysemy and word order[17], they largely treat the complex domain as a black-box feature augmentation. The geometric properties of complex num-bers—particularly the distinct roles of phase and amplitude—remain underexplored for fine-grained sentiment disentan-glement[18]. In this paper, we go beyond merely projecting features into a complex space. We explicitly assign semantic roles to the two geometric components: the phase is dedicated to modeling sentiment polarity, while the amplitude captures the lexical richness and intensity of subjective expressions. This explicit geometric decoupling is, to our knowledge, the 3A PREPRINT - M ARCH 31, 2026 first attempt to systematically exploit the phase-amplitude dichotomy for ABSA, and it proves crucial for mitigating the entanglement issue inherent in real-valued representations. 

# 3 Task Formulation & Data Construction 

3.1 Context-Aware Aspect Extraction 

Fine-grained Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis (ABSA) faces a critical challenge known as feature entanglement .In real-world reviews, a single lengthy text often contains multiple aspects with conflicting polarities (e.g., praising the food taste while complaining about the parking). To prevent the pre-trained language model from conflating these distinct sentiments, we perform a data-level disentanglement prior to representation learning[19]. Specifically, we utilize a combination of TF-IDF scored keywords and an extended expert lexicon to precisely locate aspect-specific anchors within the raw reviews. To isolate the pure sentiment associated with a specific aspect, we propose a Dynamic Window Size algorithm[20]. Instead of feeding the entire review to the model, we extract a local context block surrounding the anchor word. The window size is dynamically adjusted based on the length of the targeted segment (ranging from 2 to 4 surrounding sentences) to ensure high-recall semantic completeness while strictly excluding extraneous aspect sentiments. 

3.2 Hybrid Contrastive Triplet Generation 

To construct robust training signals for contrastive learning, we map the extracted context blocks into semantic triplets consisting of an anchor query ( xq ), a positive sample ( x+), and a negative sample ( x−). All samples within a triplet share the exact same objective aspect (e.g., “Transportation”) to control the variables. To enrich the diversity of the contrastive gradient signals, we implement a hybrid triplet generation strategy: • Standard Pairs: Mining one positive and one negative sample corresponding to the query’s polarity. • Multi-Positive/Negative Augmentation: Fusing multiple text blocks of the same polarity into a single augmented target to increase the density of the semantic space. • Hard Contrastive Mining: Selecting positive samples that share the same sentiment but exhibit high lexical variance, coercing the model to capture deep semantic alignment rather than superficial word overlap[21].

4.2 Zero-Initialized Residual Complex Projection (ZRCP) 

Recent advancements in textual similarity have demonstrated the efficacy of mapping embeddings into a complex space for angle optimization. However, standard implementations typically employ a hard chunking strategy (i.e., directly bisecting the hidden state h into real and imaginary parts). In the context of ABSA, where the model must represent 18 distinct aspect categories simultaneously, such forced bisection inherently destroys the pre-trained semantic continuity and leads to catastrophic feature entanglement. To address this limitation, we propose the Zero-Initialized Residual Complex Projection (ZRCP) module. Instead of direct bisection, we introduce two parameterized linear projections to independently model the objective aspect (mapped to the real part) and the subjective polarity (mapped to the imaginary part). 5.1 Experimental Setup Datasets We conduct our experiments on the ASAP (Aspect-based Sentiment Analysis of Restaurant Reviews) dataset. It contains highly contextualized Chinese restaurant reviews annotated with 18 fine-grained aspects, covering categories such as Location, Service, Price, Ambience, and Food. Each aspect is annotated with a sentiment polarity (Positive, Negative, or Neutral/Not-mentioned). Through our Context-Aware Aspect Extraction pipeline (Section 3.1), we generated a highly purified dataset of aspect-polarity triplets. 

Implementation Details Our model is implemented using PyTorch and the HuggingFace Transformers library. We adopt the pre-trained chinese-roberta-wwm-ext as our encoding backbone. The maximum sequence length is set to 192. We train the model for 5 epochs using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 2e-5 and a linear warmup of 500 steps. The effective batch size is set to 128 (batch size 32 with 4 gradient accumulation steps) to ensure sufficient in-batch negative sampling. For the loss function, we empirically set the weights to wibn = 1 .0, wangle = 1 .0, and 

wcos = 0 .1, with temperatures τibn = τangle = 20 . All experiments are conducted on NVIDIA GPUs with FP16 mixed precision enabled. 

Evaluation Metrics We evaluate the performance of aspect-polarity representation using Macro-F1 and Accuracy (Acc). Since fine-grained ABSA datasets typically suffer from class imbalance across aspects, Macro-F1 serves as our primary evaluation metric to ensure robust evaluation on long-tail categories.
